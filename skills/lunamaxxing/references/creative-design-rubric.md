# Creative and Product Design Rubric

Read this module for subjective visual, UI/UX, creative, redesign, “beautify,” “modernize,” or “make it professional” work.

## Reframe the request

Do not assume aesthetics are the root problem. Distinguish among hierarchy, usable space, discoverability, control placement, information density, comprehension, interaction feedback, and visual character. Inspect the existing rendered experience when available.

For deep work, generate the required safe, structural, and distinctive directions. Give each a single coherent design thesis. Do not combine them until one is selected; then borrow only compatible details that strengthen its thesis.

## Evaluate the selected direction

Assess and record actionable findings for:

- visual hierarchy and focal order;
- alignment, grid, spacing, and rhythm;
- information density and progressive disclosure;
- contrast, typography, and readability;
- discoverability and interaction affordances;
- hover, focus, selected, loading, empty, error, and disabled states;
- consistency with the existing system;
- responsive behavior and available workspace;
- long localization strings, RTL risk when relevant, and content variability;
- keyboard use, focus visibility, semantics, motion, and other accessibility needs;
- product character and memorability without sacrificing clarity.

## Required design artifact

Before implementation, state:

1. one primary design idea;
2. at least three concrete changes that express it;
3. one valuable existing trait to preserve;
4. the most plausible visual or interaction risk;
5. how the before/after difference will be observed.

After implementation, compare actual renders or artifacts against that statement. “Changed colors and spacing” is not sufficient unless evidence shows those were the root cause.
